Greek Shield: from raw option quotes to risk alerts

October 21, 2026 · 2 min read

An option’s value moves with the price of the underlying asset, the time left until expiry, volatility and interest rates, all at once. Anyone holding options needs to know how exposed they are to each of those, and they need to know it before the market moves, not after.

Greek Shield is a team project I built with Likhith Nagaralu Gurumurthy and Sneh Patel. It turns raw market data into risk signals someone can act on.

The pipeline at a glance

  1. Ingest: AAPL options data comes from Yahoo Finance.
  2. Land: raw data is stored in AWS S3.
  3. Model: it moves through six Snowflake layers, each refining the one before.
  4. Compute: Black–Scholes Greeks are calculated in the warehouse.
  5. Act: four AI agents, a Streamlit dashboard and Slack alerts turn the numbers into decisions.

Why land raw data first

Keeping an untouched copy of the source data in S3 means that when the logic changes (and it always does), we can rebuild every downstream table from the original inputs instead of hoping the old transformations were right.

Six layers, one job each

Each Snowflake layer has a single responsibility, moving from raw quotes toward analytics-ready tables. Small, single-purpose layers are easier to test, easier to debug and easier to explain to a teammate than one giant transformation.

The Greeks in one minute

  • Delta (Δ): how much the option’s price moves when the underlying moves.
  • Gamma (Γ): how quickly delta itself changes.
  • Theta (Θ): how much value the option loses as time passes.
  • Vega (ν): sensitivity to changes in volatility.
  • Rho (ρ): sensitivity to interest rates.

Black–Scholes gives closed-form formulas for these. We implemented them as Snowflake UDFs, so they’re computed right next to the data, then aggregated them into portfolio-level metrics for automated risk monitoring.

Where the AI agents fit

Numbers alone don’t tell a risk manager what to do. On top of the Greeks sits a four-agent workflow that combines Snowflake Cortex with GPT-based reasoning. Together the agents analyze risk, generate plain-language explanations, trigger Slack alerts, and surface insights in the Streamlit dashboard.

From dashboards to alerts

The Streamlit dashboard is for exploring. Slack alerts are for the moments that can’t wait: they go to where people already are, so a risk threshold being crossed doesn’t depend on someone having the dashboard open.

What I’d tell someone building something similar

  • Keep the raw data. You will need to reprocess it.
  • Compute where the data lives instead of shipping it around.
  • Make every alert specific enough that the reader knows what to do next.
